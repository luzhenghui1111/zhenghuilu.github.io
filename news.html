From Graduate School to a Postdoc: Why I Started Documenting My Research Journey从读研到博后，我想开始记录科研

From graduate school to doctoral training and now postdoctoral research, much of the journey has been made up of ordinary, repetitive work: learning methods, running experiments, debugging models, revising papers, presenting unfinished ideas, and gradually figuring out what kind of research I want to do. Looking back, I realized that many of the most useful parts of that process never appear in a CV or publication list.

从读研、读博到现在做博士后，科研经历里其实有很多并不会出现在 CV 和论文列表中的部分：学方法、做实验、调模型、改论文、参加会议，也包括很多走弯路和重新想清楚问题的过程。回头看，这些过程本身反而有不少值得留下来的东西。

That is why I want to start documenting more of the process itself: research methods I have learned, tools that proved useful, ideas that did or did not work, and occasional thoughts about academic life. The goal is not to turn every update into a polished conclusion, but to leave a record that may be useful to myself and to others working on similar problems.

所以我想开始多记录一些科研过程本身，包括学过的方法、用过的工具、做过但未必成功的尝试，以及偶尔对科研和学术生活的一些想法。这里不一定每一篇都需要有一个非常完整的结论，更重要的是把过程留下来，也希望其中有些内容能对做类似工作的朋友有用。

Making OpenSim and FEA workflows less repetitive让 OpenSim 和 FEA 流程少一点重复劳动

Much of computational biomechanics is repetitive file handling, checking, and preprocessing. Small automation tools can make the workflow more reproducible while leaving more time for the actual scientific questions.
